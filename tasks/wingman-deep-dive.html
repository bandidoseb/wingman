Live Pipeline

From spoken word
to signal card.

Here's what happens technically in the ~500 milliseconds between a prospect raising an objection and the rep seeing exactly what to say.

1
Rep clicks "Deploy Wingman" t = 0
The Electron desktop app launches the Recall.ai SDK, fetches a short-lived upload token from the Railway backend (the Recall API key never leaves the server), and joins the active Zoom, Teams, or Google Meet window.
2
Deepgram transcribes in real-time continuous
Audio capture and transcription happen entirely inside Recall.ai's infrastructure — Wingman never touches raw audio. Speaker-labeled text arrives as a stream of transcript events. Duplicate utterances are filtered before anything reaches the AI.
3
Two paths fire simultaneously
Every transcript chunk triggers two parallel checks. Neither waits for the other.
⚡ Path A — Instant
Keyword classifier
Pattern-matches prospect speech against known objection phrases. Runs in memory — no API call. Fires in under 1ms. If it hits, a pre-written signal card appears immediately and Path B is also launched.
🧠 Path B — RAG
Knowledge Base lookup
The transcript chunk is embedded (OpenAI, ~200ms), then searched against 6,500 trigger phrases using cosine similarity. The top 3 matching techniques are retrieved and sent to Claude Haiku.
4
Claude Haiku generates the card ~280ms
A ~1,300 token prompt assembles: the system prompt, the rep's Offer Brain, the top 3 KB techniques, and the trigger chunk. Haiku returns a 5–10 word headline and one-sentence subtext in JSON. The system prompt is cached for the entire call duration — reducing latency and cost by 90% on every card after the first.
5
Pusher pushes to the HUD instant
The signal card is pushed via WebSocket (Pusher) the moment it's ready — no polling, no delay. The HUD window (a transparent overlay on top of all other apps) receives it and fades it in, bottom-left. The prospect sees nothing. The rep sees exactly what to say.
6
Sonnet tracks the call in parallel every 8–90s
Separately from signal cards, Claude Sonnet receives the accumulated transcript buffer on a stage-based timer (fast during Discovery and Close, slow during Rapport). It tracks which of the 8 call stages the rep is in, updates the live scorecard, and monitors framework coverage — without interfering with the real-time signal card path.

The Moat

A brain that belongs
to the rep. Forever.

After every call, Claude Sonnet updates the rep's Offer Brain — a private AI model of their exact deal, their prospects, their patterns. It compounds silently in the background. The longer a rep uses Wingman, the more irreplaceable it becomes.

What the Offer Brain stores

🎯
Prospect archetypes
The types of buyers this rep encounters most. Pain points, hesitations, vocabulary they use.
💬
Top objections + what worked
Not just what objections appear — but which exact responses moved them past it.
💰
Price reveal patterns
At what point in the call price comes up, how it lands, and what closes after it.
⏱
Average close timing
How long this rep's calls run, which stages take longest, where deals die.
📈
Stage patterns
Where the rep tends to rush, where they stall, which transitions they miss.
🎖
What this rep does well
Wingman reinforces existing strengths — not just plugging gaps.
