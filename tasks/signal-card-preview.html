The Ambient Dot — Between Signal Cards

8px dot, bottom-left. Slow breathing pulse — 3 second cycle. The prospect cannot see this. No text.

Why a persistent anchor element?

The biggest distraction wasn't the card itself — it was the anxiety of waiting for a card to appear. Research by Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert (2015) found that merely knowing a notification might arrive causes a measurable drop in task performance, even when no notification arrives. The brain reserves attentional capacity to monitor empty space. This is called on-call attention.

The fix: give the brain something stable to monitor. When the dot is always present, the visual system switches from "vigilant watch mode" to "ambient check mode" — the same low-cost scanning behavior that lets a driver monitor their speedometer without losing focus on the road.

The slow breathing pulse (opacity 0.45 → 1.0 over 3 seconds) confirms the system is live without creating urgency. No text. No label. After a few calls it becomes invisible infrastructure — noticed only when absent.

Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert (2015) — "The attentional cost of receiving a cell phone notification." Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance. · Schneider & Shiffrin (1977) — automaticity development in attentional processing.
Full Design Spec — Scientifically Sourced
Position
Bottom-left
80px from bottom, 32px from left edge. Avoids Zoom face region (top-center) and Zoom panel zone (right side).
Z-pattern visual hierarchy · Kendon (1967) gaze aversion · EBU R37 subtitle placement standard
Entry Animation
Fade in place
200ms, ease-in curve (slow start, fast finish). No slide, no scale, no bounce. Card materializes — it does not arrive.
Franconeri & Simons (2003) · Google Glass HIG · FAA HUD Design Standard DOT/FAA/CT-96/1
Exit Animation
Fade out
150ms ease-out. Abrupt disappearance triggers the same orienting reflex as abrupt appearance.
Yantis & Jonides (1984) — onset/offset attention capture · Sokolov (1963) — orienting reflex
Background
rgba(18,18,24, 0.82)
Dark blue-gray at 82% opacity + backdrop blur. Dark overlays recede; light overlays advance. 80%+ opacity needed for readability against busy video backgrounds.
Bauer & Cavonius (1980) — polarity and visual fatigue · Automotive HUD opacity research
Headline Text
18px, weight 700
rgba(255,255,255,0.93) — near-white, not pure white. Peripheral vision has lower spatial resolution; text must be larger than it would be for direct reading.
Arditi & Cho (2005) — peripheral font size requirements · Previc (1990) — visual field asymmetries
Support Text
12px, weight 400
rgba(255,255,255,0.42), italic. Clearly subordinate. Read only during a natural pause before responding — never required on the initial glance.
Visual hierarchy principle · Wickens Multiple Resource Theory (2002) — verbal task competition
Font
System sans-serif
SF Pro on Mac. No dyslexic font — benefit is dyslexia-specific, not general. Sans-serif is measurably faster in peripheral reading vs. serif.
Arditi & Cho (2005) — serif vs. sans-serif peripheral readability
Word Count
Headline: ≤5 words
Under stress + active speech + peripheral reading: ~2–3 words/second. Headline alone must be fully actionable. Body is optional depth.
Pashler (1994) — dual-task interference · Arnsten (2009) — stress, working memory & PFC · NHTSA glance duration research
Ambient Dot
8px, always on
Bottom-left, slow breathing pulse (opacity 0.45→1.0, 3s ease-in-out loop). Never pulses faster. No text. Never disappears while Wingman is live.
Stothart et al. (2015) — on-call attention cost · Schneider & Shiffrin (1977) — automaticity
Color Coding
None — unified
All card types use identical dark style. Saturated colors (red, green, orange, blue) activate the focal attention pathway and force a hard look mid-conversation.
Treisman Feature Integration Theory — color as pre-attentive feature · Parvocellular pathway research
Card Width
280px max
Nudge cards ~220px. Objection cards up to 280px. Narrower cards minimize the eye travel required from the prospect's face.
NHTSA glance duration research — minimize time away from primary task
Border
rgba(255,255,255,0.09)
Barely visible. Defines the card edge without drawing attention. Enough contrast to separate the card from the video feed behind it.
Ambient/focal attention model — secondary info should be detectable, not demanding

Card Format by Stage
Format Rules

Rapport → Discovery → Transition: Headline only. ≤4 words. Pure directional nudge. No body text. Rep reads it in a single glance without breaking stride. Examples: "Dig deeper on pain." / "Ask about timeline." / "Slow down."

Objections → Close: Headline (≤5 words, fully actionable alone) + one italic support sentence below a faint divider. The headline is what the rep reads on the initial glance. The support line is read during the natural pause before responding — the few seconds while the rep is formulating their reply. The support line is never required; the headline always stands on its own.

The rule: If the headline alone wouldn't tell a trained rep what to do, the headline isn't tight enough yet. Rewrite the headline, not the card format.
