# Competitors

Last updated: 2026-04-02

---

## The Honest Competitive Landscape

Wingman has no direct competitor doing exactly what it does for exactly the user it serves. There are adjacent tools. None of them are built for high-ticket phone closers working on commission. Understanding why they're not real competitors is as important as knowing they exist.

---

## "Competitors" That Aren't Actually Competitors

### Gong
**What it is:** Enterprise conversation intelligence platform. Records sales calls, transcribes them, and gives managers analytics on their team's calls.
**Why it's not a real competitor:** Gong is a manager's tool, not a rep's tool. It sells to VP Sales and Revenue Operations at companies with 20+ rep sales teams. The buyer is management. The use case is "let me see what my reps are doing wrong at scale." Wingman's buyer is the individual rep. Wingman doesn't compete for the same buyer, the same use case, or the same moment in the sales process.
**Price:** $100–$200/seat/month minimum. Enterprise procurement required.
**Relevant:** Gong is what a rep's *company* might use to record and analyze calls. Wingman is what the rep personally uses to not need that analysis in the first place.

### Chorus (now ZoomInfo Conversation Intelligence)
**What it is:** Very similar to Gong. Enterprise call recording and analytics.
**Why it's not a real competitor:** Same reasons as Gong. Manager-centric, enterprise-priced, post-hoc analysis. Not real-time. Not for individual closers buying their own tools.

### Fathom
**What it is:** AI notetaker. Joins your meeting as a bot, records the call, and sends a summary/action items after.
**Why it's not a real competitor:** Fathom is a notetaker for knowledge workers managing multiple meetings. It serves the "I don't want to take notes" use case, not the "I need coaching on how to close" use case. A visible bot joining the prospect's call is antithetical to Wingman's invisible copilot approach. The Wingman user doesn't want a notetaker — they want to be coached.

### Otter.ai / Fireflies
**What it is:** AI transcription and meeting summary tools.
**Why it's not a real competitor:** Same category as Fathom. Transcription and notes, not coaching. These tools are used after a call for documentation — not during a call for performance.

### Hyperbound
**What it is:** AI roleplay tool for sales reps. Practice pitching against an AI prospect.
**Why it's not a real competitor:** Hyperbound is for practice, not live calls. It's a training tool that simulates sales conversations. Valuable for reps building new skills, but it can't help you in the moment when a real prospect says "I need to think about it." Complementary to Wingman, not competitive.

### Poised
**What it is:** Real-time communication coaching app that analyzes how you speak (filler words, pace, clarity) during video calls.
**Why it's not a real competitor:** Poised coaches communication style (how you speak), not sales methodology (what you say and when). It's used by professionals who want to be better communicators — not specifically closers trying to improve close rates. Different user, different pain, different outcome.

### Sybill
**What it is:** AI that analyzes prospect behavior on video calls (facial expressions, engagement) and generates call summaries.
**Why it's not a real competitor:** Prospect behavior analysis for managers and AEs in B2B SaaS sales. Not real-time coaching, not for high-ticket phone closers.

## What Would Actually Be a Competitive Threat

A real Wingman competitor would need to:
1. Be built specifically for high-ticket phone closers (not managers, not SDRs, not enterprise AEs)
2. Fire coaching cards in real-time, during the live call, invisibly to the prospect
3. Use the rep's own call history and offer to personalize its coaching
4. Be priced for individual reps (not enterprise seat licenses)

**Current probability of this existing:** Low. Real-time AI coaching for live calls requires Recall.ai-level infrastructure, a strong sales methodology foundation, and a clear thesis on exactly who you're building for. Most sales tech companies chase enterprise (bigger contracts, bigger logos) rather than the individual high-ticket closer. The closer market is real but looks "too small" to venture-backed companies building for enterprise.

---

## The Real Competition

The real competition for Wingman is not another software product. It is:

1. **Doing nothing.** Most closers just keep closing the way they've always closed, missing the same deals for the same reasons.
2. **Sales coaching (human).** A good sales coach costs $500–$2,000/month and gives you 1-2 hours of feedback per week. Wingman costs $149/month and gives you real-time coaching on every call.
3. **Courses and training.** One-time purchase. No ongoing coaching. No personalization. No live call help.

Wingman wins the comparison against courses and coaching on ROI per dollar. The pitch isn't "better than competitors" — it's "better than the alternative of coaching yourself without help."
