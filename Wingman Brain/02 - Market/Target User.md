# Target User

Last updated: 2026-04-02

---

## Who They Are

**The Wingman target user is a high-ticket phone closer in the info product space.**

Specifically: a commission-based sales rep who closes deals over Zoom or Google Meet, selling offers priced between $3,000 and $15,000. The offers are typically:
- Fitness coaching programs (1:1 coaching, 12-week transformations)
- Business opportunity programs (online business, agency, social media, e-commerce)
- Investing education (real estate, stocks, options, land investing)
- High-ticket consulting or masterminds

These are not B2B SaaS sales reps. They're not enterprise account executives. They're independent closers or closers employed by info product companies, paid almost entirely on commission. Their income is directly proportional to their close rate.

---

## How They Work

**Volume:** 3–8 calls per day. Some days more. Calls are typically 45–75 minutes.

**Platforms:** Zoom (primary), Google Meet (common), Microsoft Teams (rarer in this space).

**Structure:** They work from a script or framework (Cole Gordon, Jeremy Miner NEPQ, or a variation). Most have been trained — either by their company, by a paid course, or by a coach. They know the methodology. They just don't execute it consistently.

**Income:** Commission-only or base + commission. Top closers make $10k–$30k/month. Average closer makes $5k–$10k/month. Below $5k = probably not our target user (the ROI math doesn't work as cleanly).

**Prep:** Minimal to moderate. They review the lead's application or qualification form before the call. Some do a quick look at the prospect's social media. Most don't have time for deep prep — they're back-to-back.

**Review:** Most do very little call review. Watching your own call recordings is uncomfortable and time-consuming. This is exactly the gap Wingman's Flight Log fills — the debrief they'd get from a coach, without the call recording, in 30 seconds.

## What Their Calls Look Like Without Wingman

A typical closer runs through a framework they've been trained on. On good calls, they nail every stage. On bad calls, they rush Discovery, skip the Press stage, jump to pitch, and then get hammered with objections they're not prepared for.

The two most common failure patterns:
1. **Moving too fast.** Rep hears a hint of pain, immediately jumps to the solution. Prospect hasn't felt the pain deeply enough to pay for the solution. Call dies at the price reveal.
2. **Caving on objections.** Prospect says "I need to think about it." Rep gives them two weeks and loses the deal. The right move (reopen pain, find the real objection) is obvious in hindsight. In the moment, under pressure, with a live human on the other end — reps fold.

---

## Their Biggest Frustrations

1. **"I know what I should have done — but I didn't do it."** This is the most common sentiment. Closers know the methodology. They've been trained. The failure is execution under pressure, not knowledge.

2. **No feedback loop.** Their manager (if they have one) doesn't have time to review every call. Coaching sessions are weekly at best. By the time they get feedback on a call, they can't remember what happened.

3. **Inconsistency.** Great week, bad week, great week. They can't identify what they did differently. The wins feel random. The losses feel random. No pattern visibility.

4. **"I always cave at price."** The closer's most expensive moment is the 5 seconds after price reveal. Most let the silence last 2 seconds, then start talking. That talking signals insecurity and opens the negotiation.

5. **Discovery that goes nowhere.** They ask the questions, the prospect answers, but the rep doesn't know how to follow up on the answers to excavate deeper pain. The call stays surface-level. The prospect doesn't feel understood. They don't buy.

## Why They're Faster-Moving Than Enterprise Buyers

- They don't need approval to spend $149/month. It comes out of their personal income.
- They feel the pain every single working day — every missed close is money out of their pocket.
- They're already spending money on training, courses, and coaching. They have a framework for evaluating tools that improve performance.
- They're word-of-mouth heavy. One closer who sees Wingman work will tell every other closer they know.
- They have no procurement process, no IT security review, no legal sign-off. If it works, they pay for it.

## Who Is NOT the Target User

- SDRs doing cold outbound (different call structure, different pain, different KB needed)
- Appointment setters (their goal is a booking, not a close)
- B2B SaaS AEs (different sales cycle, different methodology, likely on Windows/enterprise tools)
- Enterprise sales teams (manager-level buyer, procurement process, compliance requirements)
- Beginners with <6 months of closing experience (don't know the methodology well enough to use Wingman effectively — they need a coach, not a copilot)
- Closers doing <$2k/month in commissions (ROI math breaks down — the subscription is too large a percentage of income)
