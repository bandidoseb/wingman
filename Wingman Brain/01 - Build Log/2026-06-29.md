## The data-driven prompt decision (eval results)

Ran the eval multiple times (variance matters — single runs are noisy; the AI judge is itself stochastic):

- **Keep the CURRENT prompt's tonality — the v2 rewrite never beat it** (tied across runs, v1 even edged it). BUT v1
  lacked an explicit no-fabrication / number-confidence rule (those were only in v2). → shipped a **MERGE**: v1 tonality
  body + a spliced "USING THEIR DETAILS" accuracy fence. Holds tonality (within noise), 0 fabrications across 6 runs.
- **Number safety = scrub UNCONFIRMED digits at storage** — proved 6 leaks → 0 once shaky digits aren't stored.
- **Anchors (the "it remembered me" library) = DEFERRED, not killed.** Aggregate lift is within judge noise; the win is
  per-scenario (a kill-shot detail like "you said you can't go back to a 9-5"). AND anchors carried a fabrication tail
  (1 bad card in 3 runs) until we found + fixed the root cause.
- **Caching is OUT** — Sonnet 4.6's min cacheable prompt is **2,048 tokens** (NOT the ~1,024 I first told Sebastian — that's
  older Sonnet); our prompt is ~1,700, so it wouldn't fire, and the savings would be tiny anyway. Don't pad to reach it.

### The fabrication root cause (important)
Both bad cards were the SAME move: **inventing social proof about OTHER people** — "the coaches who hit $X." Not
"misused the prospect's number" — manufactured third-party claims. My first fence only forbade inventing the *prospect's*
details. Added one targeted rule ("NEVER invent claims about other people / manufacture social proof") → **0 fabrications
across 3 re-runs** (was 1/3). Sebastian was right: a text rule CAN catch it, and a runtime validator would've been the
wrong tool anyway (the leaked number was real; the invented part was the claim — a number-validator passes it). Also
added a "when to anchor" rule (only when a real detail sharpens the move; not every objection needs one).
