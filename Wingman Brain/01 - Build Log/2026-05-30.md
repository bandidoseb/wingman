### 2. Groq Research — Should We Switch from Claude Haiku?
Sebastian asked about Groq (chip company, not Grok/Elon's chatbot) after learning it runs inference 5-10x faster and cheaper. Ran two full research agents — first with 2024 data (flagged as stale), second with 2026-only sources.

**Conclusion: Stay on Claude Haiku 4.5 for now.**
- Groq TTFT for Llama 4 Scout: 0.81s independently verified (Artificial Analysis). That's nearly our entire 1s budget before a single token is generated.
- For 30-token outputs, Groq's throughput advantage disappears — TTFT is what matters, and it's tight.
- Claude Haiku 4.5 Arena Elo: 1,404 (Feb 2026). Best open-source competitor (Llama 4 Maverick): ~1,380. Claude wins on quality.
- JSON reliability: Groq 98.1% vs Claude 99.3% (May 29, 2026 production report). In a live call, a 1-in-50 malformed card is a visible failure.
- Cost delta at current scale: <$1/day. Not worth the quality risk during beta.
- **Revisit post-beta at real user volume.**

### 3. Prompt Caching Deep Dive
Discovered that `knowledge-signal.ts` (the live signal card generator — most latency-sensitive endpoint) has **zero prompt caching**. The other routes (poll, chat) do have it.

**1-hour TTL cache confirmed real:** `cache_control: { type: "ephemeral", ttl: "1h" }`. Costs 2x to write, 0.1x to read (90% off). For a 60-min call with 10 signal cards: pay 2x once, then 0.1x nine times. Big net savings.

**Cache warming concept:** Fire a dummy request to Claude at call start (when session begins) to warm the cache before the first signal card trigger lands. Makes Card #1 as fast as Card #10. Added to sprint as item 0i.
