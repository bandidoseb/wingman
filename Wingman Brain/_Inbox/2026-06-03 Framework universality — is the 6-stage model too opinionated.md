# Open product question — is the 6-stage framework too opinionated? (2026-06-03, Sebastian)

**Concern (Sebastian):** Not every rep thinks of their calls as Rapport → Discovery → Press → Confirm → Pitch → Close. Different schools use different names/sequences. If Wingman scores a rep on a framework they don't use, it feels like being graded on a stranger's checklist → eye-roll → churn.

## The reframe (Claude's take)
- The **concepts are universal**; the **labels + rigid sequence are the opinionated part.** Every high-ticket closer (NEPQ, Sandler, Challenger, Cole Gordon, homegrown) builds trust, finds pain, builds urgency, confirms, pitches, closes — they just name/order it differently.
- The "not how I sell" friction comes from (1) jargon labels — esp. "Press" and "Confirm" (Cole-flavored), and (2) implying a strict 1→6 order.

## Recommendation (keep the spine, soften the imposition)
1. **Lead with topic coverage, not sequence.** We already have two scores: 6 **stages** (sequence, opinionated) and 7 **pillars** (Core Problem, Can't DIY, Cost of Waiting, Dream Outcome, Investment, Who's Deciding, Why You — flow-agnostic, score *what* not *when*). Make the **pillars/topic-coverage the headline**; demote the rigid stage order to secondary context. Topic coverage is what any methodology agrees on.
2. **Plain language for labels.** Keep universal ones (Rapport, Discovery, Pitch, Close); soften jargon ("Press" → "Cost of Inaction / Urgency"; "Confirm" → "Confirm Before Pitch").
3. **Keep the opinionated spine — it's a beachhead feature.** First users = Cole Gordon / Closers.io world, literally trained on this framework. Native + a selling point for them. Don't water it to mush (a POV is what makes coaching sharp).
4. **Long-term answer = personalization (already roadmapped):**
   - **Playbook** (planned) → rep tweaks their framework/language once.
   - **Sales DNA** (big build) → *learns each rep's actual flow from their calls* and adapts the framework to how THEY sell. Becomes "my method, sharpened," not "Wingman's method." This is the moat.

## Bottom line
Keep framework as the spine; **lead with universal topic-coverage + plain language**; personalize over time via Playbook/Sales DNA. Meet reps where they are, then guide.

## RESOLUTION (2026-06-03, worked through with Sebastian) — "layer, don't choose"
The simple-vs-expert tension resolves by layering, not compromising:

1. **Show the 4 everyone agrees on: Rapport · Discovery · Pitch · Close.** Universal buy-in, zero "that's not how I sell" friction.
2. **Press + Confirm become depth INSIDE Discovery, not top-level boxes.** "Press" is already the `cost_of_waiting` pillar; "Confirm" is the transition beat → delivered as coaching within Discovery ("found the pain but never made waiting expensive" / "pitched without confirming the transition"). The elite standard is still taught — as depth, not alien jargon.
3. **Hold the standard — do NOT dumb it down.** The opinionated bar IS the product; a coach that mirrors your habits back is worthless. Universal *surface* ≠ lowered *bar*. Removing jargon friction lets the standard land; it doesn't weaken it.
4. **Sales DNA flaw (Sebastian's catch): learning a rep's flow could codify a BAD process.** Fix = clean split: **Sales DNA personalizes CONTENT** (offer, prospect types, real objections, winning language) — **the FRAMEWORK stays the fixed standard.** DNA never decides "this rep skips the close, so stop grading it." Student doesn't grade their own exam. Content adapts to their business; the bar does not bend.

**Bottom line:** one opinionated standard · universal surface (4 stages) · elite depth underneath (pillars) · personalize the content, never the standard.

**Status:** framing/copy decision — NOT a code blocker (Phase 1 produces the data regardless). Shapes the post-call viz redesign + the Playbook build. The "show 4, coach the rest as depth" model + the "DNA personalizes content not standard" rule are the locked direction. Revisit when building the viz/Playbook.
