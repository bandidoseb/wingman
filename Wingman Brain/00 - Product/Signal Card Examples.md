# Signal Card Examples

Last updated: 2026-04-10

A living library of signal card examples organized by trigger type and call stage. These are reference examples — what a signal card should look and feel like. As beta data comes in, replace synthetic examples with real ones that landed well.

---

## Card Format (updated 2026-04-10)

Two-tier format, rules vary by stage:

**Early stages (Rapport → Situation → Desired Outcome → Transition):**
- Headline only. ≤4 words. Pure directional nudge. No support text.
- Rep reads it in a single glance without breaking stride.

**Late stages (Pain → Close + Objections):**
- **Headline:** ≤5 words. Fully actionable alone. Rep reads this on initial glance.
- **Support:** One italic sentence. An example question or framing for the rep to use during the natural pause before responding. Optional — headline must work without it.

**The rule:** If the headline alone wouldn't tell a trained rep what to do, the headline isn't tight enough. Rewrite the headline, not the card.

**Format for each entry:**
- **Trigger:** The exact moment/words that caused the card to fire
- **Headline:** ≤5 words (shown large, bold — read in one glance)
- **Support:** One sentence (shown small, italic — read during pause before responding)
- **Why it works:** The principle behind the card

## Objections — Close Stage

### "That's a lot of money"
**Trigger:** Prospect says some version of "that's a lot," "that's more than I expected," or long silence after price reveal.

**Headline:** *Reopen the pain.*
**Support:** *"What has staying stuck cost you over the last 6 months?"*

**Why it works:** Defending the price is the instinct. It's also wrong. When you defend, you're arguing. When you ask about cost-of-status-quo, you're returning to pain — which is what got them interested in the first place. The prospect is comparing $X to their bank account. Wingman's job is to redirect the comparison to what staying stuck costs them every month.

---

### "I need to think about it"
**Trigger:** Prospect uses "think about it," "sleep on it," or "give me some time" after price is revealed.

**Headline:** *Find the real objection.*
**Support:** *"What specifically is making you hesitate?"* Then go quiet.

**Why it works:** "I need to think about it" almost never means they need time. It means they have an unspoken objection. The rep's job is to find it. Pushing them to decide faster creates resistance. Asking what's specifically making them hesitate surfaces the real objection so it can be handled.

---

### "I need to talk to my spouse / partner"
**Trigger:** Prospect raises a third-party decision maker late in the call.

**Headline:** *What will they say?*
**Support:** *"When you say talk to [partner] — what do you think they're going to say?"*

**Why it works:** This is a pattern interrupt. Most prospects use "talk to my spouse" as a soft exit. Asking what the spouse will say forces them to articulate the real objection. If the spouse is genuinely the blocker, the rep now knows and can handle it. If it's an excuse, the prospect's answer will reveal that too.

---

### "I'm not sure if now is the right time"
**Trigger:** Prospect expresses timing hesitation — "maybe in a few months," "let me get through Q1 first," etc.

**Headline:** *What does waiting cost?*
**Support:** *"What do you lose if this doesn't change in the next 90 days?"*

**Why it works:** The Gordon Gap — quantify the distance between where they are and where they want to be, multiplied by time. Loss aversion is stronger than opportunity gain in human psychology. "What does waiting cost?" forces the prospect to calculate the cost of inaction, not just the cost of the program.

---

### After price reveal — rep speaks first
**Trigger:** Rep reveals price and then immediately speaks — doesn't allow silence.

**Headline:** *Stop talking. Wait.*
**Support:** *(No support text — the directive is complete. Silence is the technique.)*

**Why it works:** After price reveal, silence is the rep's most powerful weapon. The impulse to fill silence with justification ("but it includes...") signals insecurity and invites negotiation. Staying quiet signals confidence. The prospect will break the silence with either a yes, an objection (handleable), or a question (also handleable).

## Discovery — Pain Stage

### Prospect volunteers a big problem, rep moves on
**Trigger:** Prospect mentions something painful ("I lose deals every time I have to discuss price") and the rep immediately moves to the next question.

**Headline:** *Go back. Dig deeper.*
**Support:** *"You mentioned [prospect's exact words] — can you tell me more about that?"*

**Why it works:** Reps trained to cover a list of questions often skip past the biggest pain because they're moving to the next item. The signal card catches this and uses the prospect's exact words to create a natural pivot. The exact words carry emotional weight that a summary doesn't.

---

### Rep is moving through Situation too fast
**Trigger:** Rep has spent less than 5 minutes in Discovery and is already transitioning toward pitch.

**Headline:** *Slow down.*
**Support:** *"What have you already tried to solve this?"*

**Why it works:** "What have you already tried?" surfaces emotional investment and qualifies the problem as real. If they've tried nothing, the pain isn't urgent. If they've tried multiple things, the pain is deep. Rushing to the solution before understanding the problem is the most common mistake Wingman is designed to catch.

## Pre-Pitch / Transition Stage

### Rep about to pitch without confirming
**Trigger:** Rep uses transition language ("so what I'd love to show you is...") without having completed a Confirm step.

**Headline:** *Confirm before pitching.*
*(No support text — this is a nudge stage card.)*

**Why it works:** Pitching before confirming is the second most expensive mistake in a sales call. The prospect is thinking about something you didn't address, and you're presenting a solution to a problem they've already mentally moved past. The Confirm step is 30 seconds and kills ghost objections.

---

### Rep jumping straight from Discovery to Pitch
**Trigger:** Rep completes Discovery questions and immediately begins presenting the offer without a Bridge.

**Headline:** *Summarize their pain first.*
*(No support text — nudge only.)*

**Why it works:** The Bridge proves the rep was listening — which builds massive trust — and anchors the pitch to the specific problem they described. A pitch without a Bridge feels like a PowerPoint. A pitch after a Bridge feels like a prescription.

## Framework Coverage

### Rep never pressed on cost of inaction
**Trigger:** Transcript analysis shows the call went from Pain to Pitch without a Press stage.

**Headline:** *Quantify the cost.*
**Support:** *"What does this cost you every month you don't fix it?"*

**Why it works:** The Press stage is the most commonly skipped and the most important for high-ticket closing. Reps who skip Press get to Close and find the prospect isn't emotionally ready — because the cost of inaction never felt real to them.

## Notes on Card Language (updated 2026-04-10)

- **Headline is the complete message.** If the rep reads only the headline and responds, the card worked.
- **Support is optional depth.** Written for the pause before responding — never required.
- Cards must sound like the rep's inner voice, not a textbook.
- Never use jargon the rep wouldn't say themselves ("leverage the NEPQ framework").
- Use the prospect's exact words in the support text whenever possible — in quotes.
- Phrase headlines as a directive, not a suggestion ("Reopen the pain." not "Consider reopening...")
