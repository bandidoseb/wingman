# Live-Call Findings — JP Egan, Johnny Mau, Christian Polk, Jeremy Miner, Tim Ray

Source: deep-read of 13 LIVE-CALL transcripts in `live-calls/` (8 JP Egan, 1 Johnny Mau,
2 Christian Polk, 1 Jeremy Miner, 1 Tim Ray). These are real closers handling objections on
real prospects — vs the 7 training videos (Cole/Ryder) which teach the theory.

## Headline: live practice CONFIRMS the architecture
Nothing here says "change the approach." The live calls reinforce, in the wild, everything in
`PLAYBOOK-SYNTHESIS.md`:
- **The objection handler is the cash-out of discovery.** Christian Polk says it on-camera:
  *"now what I have is leverage… if you don't have problems, when they say 'I just need to think
  about it,' you don't have anything to pull on."* That IS the Wingman thesis, from a practitioner.
- The universal spine is identical to Cole/Ryder: **pace → tie-down → get them to say WHY it works
  → isolate → handle.**

## What's UNIVERSAL across every closer (the general concept)
1. **The tie-down is the same move, every time:** *"[objection] aside — do you feel like this can
   actually get you to [their outcome]?"* → then *"why?"* Every single closer (Egan, Mau, Polk,
   Miner, Ray) opens objection handling with this. It is THE first move. (Note: live closers use
   this simpler, warmer "do you feel it can get you there?" form MORE than Cole's "anything keeping
   you from 100% certain" form — same function, more natural out loud.)
2. **Get them to re-establish value BEFORE handling anything.** Tim Ray, explicitly: *"I don't
   handle any objection before the prospect tells me why they think it'll work."* The "why?" makes
   THEM rebuild the case (consistency bias).
3. **Drill the emotional why:** *"what does that do for you beyond putting zeros in the bank?"* →
   3–4 "why" layers deep. Universal (Egan + Polk go deepest).
4. **Cost of inaction, quantified.** Polk does the math live: *"$18k/mo × 3 months = $54k left on
   the table — does that number sound right?"* Egan quantifies missed sales constantly.
5. **Future-self / perspective reframe:** decisions from where you want to be, not where you are
   (= Cole/Ryder circumstances-vs-vision). Universal.
6. **Commitment tie-down before price:** *"are you committed to doing everything necessary?"*
7. **"I can help with the investment; what I can't help with is the commitment."** (Egan + Polk,
   near-verbatim.)
8. **Money reframe:** *"are you viewing the money as a problem or a symptom? If you have the skills,
   what comes with the skills? Money."* (Egan signature.)
9. **Open-wallet financial:** *"what do you have available — all accounts, be real with me"* → add
   up → highest payment plan → trade. (Mau, almost exactly like Cole.)
10. **"Two types of people"** binary as the closing frame — near-universal.

## The VARIANCE (everyone has their own style — what to learn from each)
- **JP Egan** — identity/perspective philosophy; relentless multi-turn looping through reframes;
  heavy ANALOGIES (skydiving, Harvard, gym, valley); warm, agreeable, validating; grinds an
  objection across many turns. Spouse → "your responsibility as the provider" frame (every time).
- **Christian Polk** — most emotional + discovery-deep; drills the dream (the beach, "feel the
  sun"); does the cost-of-inaction MATH; **asks permission before every reframe** ("can I share a
  perspective?" / "can I make a suggestion?") — very disarming; tells his OWN story; "the 2k version
  of you vs the 20k version" identity shift; the "lion chasing you / what are you running from."
- **Johnny Mau** — clean textbook: "has there ever been a perfect time?"; fat-person analogy;
  valley/summit; "riskier to do nothing"; "what would you do if you knew you wouldn't fail?" →
  self-belief; open-wallet financial.
- **Jeremy Miner** (the actual source of NEPQ) — short, surgical; the AC-in-Arizona-NECESSITY
  analogy for spouse; **"am I doing this TO you or FOR you?"** (disarms the meta-callout); calm,
  cheeky; reframes spouse as necessity + the rep's responsibility.
- **Tim Ray** — narrates his own psychology, most explicit. Unique adds:
  - **Magnify the feelings** — sink INTO the negative ("that's tough, man"), do NOT laugh when they
    nervously chuckle; amplify the desire on the positive. Emotional amplification is deliberate.
  - **Don't try to make them comfortable** — your job is to help + get paid, not to be liked.
  - **Crossroads questions** — yes/no questions that force a commitment to a path.
  - **Sell their DESIRE, not your program** — "that's a lot of money" → "yes, and what YOU want is
    also a lot [of value]."
  - **Word choice: "can/will," not "could"** — more certain.
  - **3 protocols: fear / finance / partner** (think-about-it doesn't even need one).

## → Refinements to fold into the Wingman objection prompt (additive, not structural)
1. **Permission-to-reframe softener.** Add to tonality doctrine: prefer opening a reframe with
   *"can I share a perspective?" / "can I make a suggestion?"* — it's the single most disarming move
   the live closers use (Polk, Ray). Makes the line land human, never combative.
2. **Lean the tie-down to the warmer live form:** *"[X] aside, do you feel this gets you to
   [outcome]? … why?"* — and explicitly drive the "why" so the prospect re-establishes value.
3. **Magnify fear instead of rushing to logic** — when the root is fear, sink into it / name it
   before reframing (Ray), don't paper over it with a quick logical comeback.
4. **Handle the meta-callout** ("you're just using sales tactics on me") with the *"am I doing this
   to you or for you?"* move (Miner/Egan).
5. **De-gender the "your responsibility" spouse frame.** The live version ("be the man, it's your
   responsibility, don't put the burden on her") is powerful but gendered. Capture the UNDERLYING
   move — *it's YOUR decision to make, don't defer it; they'll love the outcome* — in a
   gender-neutral way.
6. **Analogies are a real tool — but they belong in the BACK-AND-FORTH, not the first card.**
   Every closer leans on analogies (skydiving/Harvard/gym/valley/AC/diet-plan) for the DEEP
   fear/uncertainty beats. But they're 2–3 sentences — too long for the one-line first tie-down.
   → Note for the multi-turn/threading session: later beats can offer an analogy-based reframe.
   (Logged in `Wingman Brain/_Inbox/multi-turn objection handling.md`.)

## What did NOT change
The core: discovery-anchored, uncertainty-first, pace-then-tie-down, lean on their own words, never
combative, one human line. The live calls validate it. Refinements above are tonal/additive.
