# Objection Handling Playbook — Cole Gordon + Matthew Ryder (distilled)

Source: deep-read of 7 transcripts (4 Cole Gordon, 3 Matthew Ryder) in this folder.
This is the distilled METHOD that the Wingman objection-handling prompt is built from.
It maps 1:1 onto Wingman's existing Belief Ladder summary (`belief-ladder.ts`).

---

## 1. What an objection actually is

- **Cole:** any reason — truthful or not, smoke screen or not — that a prospect won't move
  forward after you make the offer/ask.
- **Ryder:** a logistical barrier (real OR perceived) that stops them moving forward. He splits it:
  - **Objection** = a barrier to handle.
  - **Concern** = just a question needing a straight answer (proof / trust / gap). Answer it
    plainly — do NOT go into "sales-guy mode."
  - **No need** = bad fit, not an objection. Too bad, move on.

## 2. WHY objections happen (Cole's 4 root causes)

1. **Logistics** — they're bought in; a real-world thing blocks now (money in a 401k = 3 days,
   genuinely needs spouse, real timing). *Once the front end is mastered, 80–90% of objections
   are just logistics.*
2. **Breakdown in the sales process** = you failed to build one of the **7 beliefs**. This is THE
   one Wingman can exploit, because each unbuilt belief produces a predictable objection:
   - **Pain** not built → "I don't think I need this."
   - **Doubt** (can't DIY) not built → "I can do this myself."
   - **Cost** (of inaction) not built → "I'll do it in 3 months / let me think about it."
   - **Desire** weak → no urgency.
   - **Money** not built → financial objection.
   - **Support** not built → "I need to talk to my spouse/partner."
   - **Trust** not built → "I've been burned / heard this before / send a proposal / I need research."
3. **Conditioned buyer's response** — a script they always run ("I'm 100% in, I just always sleep
   on it"). Handle gently, don't fight the script.
4. **Unclosable** — genuinely can't afford it. The Open-Wallet technique reveals this.

## 3. The 3 buckets + the hierarchy (both teach this)

A pyramid: **Uncertainty at the top, Financial + Support at the bottom.**

- **Uncertainty-based (the critical one):** anything keeping them <100% certain that *this is the
  right thing AND now is the right time.* (think about it, research, due diligence, talk to a
  client, too risky, is there a guarantee, how do I know it'll work, sleep on it, "too expensive"
  when it's really doubt.)
- **Financial:** smoke-screen (really uncertainty) / logistics (real money movement) / condition
  (unclosable).
- **Support:** deferring to someone not on the call — smoke-screen / permission / courtesy
  (spouse, partner, board, team, boss).

**Ryder's hierarchy:** money-logistical is almost always the real root ("think about it" = think
about spending the money; "talk to my partner" = talk about the money). So when hit with a big
vague blast, **default to money first.**

## 4. THE MASTER MOVE (the heart of the whole system)

> **Cole's #1 breakthrough: NO MATTER what objection you get, ALWAYS assume it's an uncertainty
> objection FIRST. Handle uncertainty, THEN handle financial or support.** Until they're 100%
> certain it's the right thing + the right time, handling the surface objection is pointless.

The sequence (Cole and Ryder are nearly identical):

1. **Pace the first objection** — diffuse pressure. **"No problem."** (Pace-Pace-Lead, NOT a
   rebuttal. Never argue. Agree / acknowledge / appreciate, THEN lead.)
2. **Tie down on certainty** — isolate whether it's real:
   - Cole: *"No problem. Just so I can stay organized — [objection] aside, is there anything else
     keeping you from being less than 100% certain that [the method/process] is what you need to
     get to [their outcome]?"*
   - Ryder (minimal words): *"Money aside, do you feel like this would be the answer?" → "Why is
     that?" → "What is it specifically that's the key to the castle for you?" → "Why that?"*
     (each probe makes them re-establish value — consistency bias; they convince themselves.)
3. **Watch tonality.** "I think so" / hesitation is NOT a yes. **Call it out:** *"That doesn't
   sound 100% — let's just be honest, what's really going on?"* (Calling it out builds trust and
   surfaces the REAL objection.)
4. **Double tie-down** — absolute language: *"So [objection] aside, you're 100% in?"* Forces out
   any hidden uncertainty.
5. **Isolate** — now you know if it was a smoke-screen or real, and what the real one is.
   (Tools: **Scale 1–10** — "1 = I hate this/want off the phone, 10 = exactly what I need — where
   are you?" then "what's keeping you from a 9 or 10?")
6. **Handle / reframe / trade** the real objection.

**Ryder's discipline:** during this, the prospect throws OTHER objections (partner, timing). You
**note them mentally but IGNORE them** and ram the money/certainty line to a yes first. Resolve one
line fully before moving — don't get 50% through money, jump to partner, 30% through that, etc.

## 5. The tie-down language (verbatim, reusable)

- **"No problem."** (universal opener — diffuses pressure)
- **"Just so I can stay organized on my end…"** (bridge into the tie-down)
- **"[X] aside, is there anything else keeping you from being less than 100% certain that [method]
  is what you need to get to [outcome]?"** (the master tie-down)
- **"Let's just pretend money is out of the equation…"** (hypothetical → easier to isolate)
- **"That doesn't sound 100% certain — what's really going on?"** (tonality call-out)
- **"So [X] aside, you're 100% in?"** (double tie-down)
- **"How vs Should":** *"You're not in a 'should I do this' place — you're in a 'how do I make this
  work' place, right?"*

## 6. Handling the real objection (by type)

- **Fear / "big decision" / nerves / "what if it doesn't work":** give them the label (*"is it
  just a big decision? a little bit of nerves?"*), then *"Good — it's GOOD that you're nervous,
  nothing great was ever built by someone who wasn't a little nervous… but deep down you know it's
  the right thing,"* then the **"who do you want to be"** close. For "won't work for me": "are you
  going to put in the work? raise your hand when you need help? then why wouldn't it work for you?"
- **"Been burned" / tried before:** go BACK into discovery — what did you try, what was the method,
  what was missing? 3 reasons things fail: bad methodology / bad support+execution / they didn't do
  the work. Then differentiate your method. *"One bad decision is the tuition you pay for a better
  one next time — don't let it sabotage a great decision."*
- **Time / "too busy":** the **self-perpetuating loop** — *"the very problem you got on this call to
  solve (no time / no leads) is the thing keeping you from fixing it. If lead-gen is the #1
  constraint and this is the fix, what else would you spend time on?"*
- **"Think about it" / research / sleep on it:** isolate to a specific (vague → specific). Assume
  they secretly doubt it works. *"When we talk tomorrow, if you wake up feeling the same, we're
  100% moving forward, right?"* (presuppose the 24h follow-up).
- **Spouse / partner (support):** is this **courtesy** (decided, just informing) or **permission**
  (real gate)? Anything not a clear "I'm 100% doing it" = permission. Then coach the **two
  conversations**: the wimpy version vs the confident-leader version — *"which of those two guys
  convinces their spouse?"* Then **deposit close** (refundable, both join onboarding call) or
  **follow-up close** (with partner on the call). Coach them to talk PROBLEMS not solutions.
- **Financial (real):** Open Wallet — get permission → next-30-day cash flow → cash on hand →
  credit → add up resources → offer the highest viable payment plan → **Trade**.
- **Guarantee / "is it risky":** *"is there a reason you're asking?"* — usually it's "been burned"
  or pure curiosity. Don't over-handle.

## 7. The Trade + closing mechanics

- **The Trade:** *"I don't think the best thing for you is [drop 12k / zero out savings]; I also
  don't think the best thing is to do nothing [stay stuck — reference their pain]. So what I'd be
  willing to do is [better terms]. If I'm willing to do that for you, are you willing to move
  forward right now?"* → trade better terms for a decision NOW. **Always get something back** (a
  decision now, a case study). When you go lower, ask for MORE (3-pay + a case study).
- **Circumstances vs Vision** (both): *"Stop making decisions from your current circumstances —
  make them from your vision. Decide from circumstances and you get more of the same circumstances."*
- **Payment-plan rule:** pitch the most expensive version as ONE number; never mention payment
  plans/financing in the pitch (it induces the "what are the plans?" objection). Start high, drop
  controlled (2-pay → 3-pay), offer paid-in-full incentive AFTER, never a payment-plan penalty.

## 8. Tonality & mindset (load-bearing for "keep it human, never robotic")

- **Cole's "oak tree":** grounded, centered, rooted. Don't get defensive when resistance hits.
  EXPECT 2–3 objections before the yes (avoids the "energy dump" → defensiveness).
- **Ryder's detachment:** *you cannot MAKE anyone buy.* Drop the ego. **Frustration is the #1 reason
  reps fail** — it puts venom in the tone. Be genuinely curious, never combative. *"You're looking
  at their life through a 45-minute keyhole — you don't know what's best for them."*
- **Same side of the table** — two people solving a problem, not adversaries.
- **Minimal words** (Ryder) — every extra word muddles meaning. *"Money aside, do you feel this is
  the answer?"* beats word salad. Softeners ("maybe, I guess, possibly") lower the sales-rep veil.
- **Curiosity as cover** — "help me understand…" lets you ask pointed questions without combat.

## 9. Prevention > handling (both hammer this)

- The best objection handling is **discovery**. Build all 7 beliefs + collect the anchors and you
  get logistics, not objections.
- **Use their own words.** Write down key phrases so any objection can be made incongruent with
  what they already said: *"Earlier you told me [X] — now you're saying [Y]. What's really going
  on?"* (Cialdini consistency bias.) This is THE highest-leverage handle, and Wingman already
  stores these as verbatim quotes.
- **Pre-handle** (Ryder) — raise likely objections (partner, holiday/timing) during the pitch.

## 10. Cole vs Ryder — the style range (so the prompt learns the SKILL, not one man's tics)

- **Cole:** structured, scripted, hard "100% certain" tie-downs, double tie-down, the Trade,
  aggressive financial deep-dive. More directive.
- **Ryder:** conversational, curious, minimal-words, detachment-first, "why is that / key to the
  castle / why that" probing, ignore-other-objections-ram-money, partner decision-tree. Softer,
  more EQ.
- **Both agree on the spine:** pace ("no problem") → "[X] aside" tie-down → isolate → same side of
  the table → handle (fear reframe, circumstances-vs-vision, the loop) → trade. Money is the most
  common root. Never rebut. Anchor to what they said.

## 11. → What the Wingman objection card must do (the translation)

When the rep presses for objection help, the card should:

1. **Diagnose the belief.** Map the live objection to the weak belief from the Belief Ladder
   (`⚠️ at-risk` / Weakest Link). That tells us what's actually unbuilt.
2. **Default to uncertainty first** (Cole's #1 rule). Unless it's clearly pure logistics, the right
   FIRST move is the **pace + tie-down**, not a clever rebuttal. So the card usually hands the rep
   the *isolation question*, anchored to the prospect's stated outcome.
3. **Anchor to what they said.** Pull the gap, the emotional why, and a verbatim quote from the
   summary so the line lands on their own words ("earlier you said…").
4. **One human line, in the rep's voice.** Pace energy ("no problem"), curious not combative,
   minimal words. Never a script, never a rebuttal, never robotic.
5. **Right step for the moment.** Wingman gives ONE card per press; the sequence (pace → tie-down →
   isolate → handle → trade) is multi-step. The card gives the best NEXT move — almost always the
   pace+tie-down/isolation anchored to context, because that's what roots out whether the objection
   is even real.

This is the difference between a generic objection tool (spits a rebuttal) and Wingman (gives the
exact tie-down a top closer would say, anchored to this specific prospect's words).
